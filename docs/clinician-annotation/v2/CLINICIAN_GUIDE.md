# EMS review v2

Please start with **Cases 1–3**, then discuss whether the questions make sense before continuing.

We want your independent judgment on fictional EMS reports for a class/hackathon prototype. These answers are for evaluation, not real patient care or model-weight training. Only answer within your experience; “Cannot assess” is useful.

## Patient review

Read the EMS Field Report, then any Updated EMS Field Report. Earlier reports appear first. `T=0` means the latest supplied report; earlier times are relative to that report, not actual clock times.

Fill the yellow boxes:

1. **EMS to ED Handoff:** What would you communicate to the receiving team? Use only the supplied facts. There is no fixed sentence count.
2. **Meaningful change:** Yes, No, Unclear, or No earlier report. If Yes, choose whether it improved, worsened, was mixed, was another change, or was unclear. A changed measurement is not automatically confirmed deterioration.
3. **Evidence / missing information:** Briefly identify the facts supporting your answer and any information you would need. “None identified” or “Cannot assess because…” is fine.
4. **Attention before arrival:** Choose one option, then briefly explain your reason or limits:
   - **Immediate review:** The receiving team should review this report now, before arrival.
   - **Review soon:** Prompt review at the next opportunity is appropriate.
   - **Routine awareness:** Keep visible; no additional immediate review is indicated by the supplied report.
   - **Cannot assess:** Information is insufficient or the judgment is outside your expertise.

These are draft review-attention labels, not formal triage levels, treatment instructions, or bed assignments. If the distinctions are not meaningful to you, note that in Form feedback rather than forcing an answer.

Age and sex were not supplied in these cases. Do not invent them, medication doses, normal test results, or other details. Tell us if missing information prevents a judgment. Keep proposed case improvements on **Form feedback** instead of adding them to the handoff.

## Attention exercise (optional)

Each group contains three inbound patients. Who should the receiving team review first for planning, using the reports and ETAs?

- Use **1, 2, or 3**. The lowest number means first to review. Equal numbers mean an intentional tie.
- Choose **Cannot assess** when an order cannot be justified.
- Say if ranking itself is not meaningful in that scenario. All patients can receive care concurrently; this does not order their treatment or exclude anyone from care.
- Give a brief reason for each patient's position or tie. Staffing, beds, activation status, and existing ED demand are not supplied; do not assume them.

There is no clinician-facing completion-status question. The team tracks completeness and approval separately. Thank you for keeping form suggestions separate from patient answers.
